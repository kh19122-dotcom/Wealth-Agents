## TL;DR (fill in)
South Korea delivered a strong trade performance in January, with the surplus remaining
high at $8.7 billion despite easing from $12.2 billion in December. Exports surged 33.9%
year on year, supported by more working days versus a year earlier and solid
semiconductor shipments. Imports rose 11.7%. We expect export growth to stay robust in
early 2026 on the back of sustained semiconductor demand, although U.S. tariff risks and
broader global trade frictions warrant close monitoring.

## Key points (fill in)

The Numbers
South Korea posted a trade surplus of $8.7 billion in January. Exports had their
strongest ever January, thanks to more working days and robust semiconductor
demand.
Exports rose 33.9% y/y after rising 13.3% in December. Imports climbed 11.7% after
rising 4.6% in the prior month.
Across major export categories, a 102.7% year-on-year jump in semiconductors was
noteworthy. Automobiles, another key export category, rose 21.7%. Machinery rose
8.6%, but petrochemicals dropped 1.5%.
Exports to major trading partners rose, including to China (up 46.7%), the U.S. (up
29.5%), the EU27 (up 6.9%), and to ASEAN (up 40.7%).
Behind the Numbers
South Korea started 2026 on a solid footing, recording its 12th consecutive monthly trade
surplus in January. That said, the headline strength should not be interpreted as a strong
turnaround. January’s strong export print was amplified by favourable calendar effects in
a month that typically sees softer performances. This helped exports post their strongest
January growth and extend their expansion to an eighth consecutive month.
Semiconductors were again the linchpin of the trade performance, with artificial
intelligence-related demand buoying shipments of advanced chips. While automobiles
and other manufactured goods also registered gains, momentum outside semiconductors
was uneven. These sectors face a 15% U.S. tariff burden, which constrains
competitiveness. We expect export growth to carry through the early months of 2026.
Robust semiconductor demand will provide support amid limited gains in other
segments.
Trade policy risks are elevated. The October U.S.
–South Korea trade agreement lowering
U.S. auto tariffs to 15% from 25% provides partial relief, but the burden remains
substantial given South Korea’s heavy reliance on U.S.
-bound shipments. U.S. President
Donald Trump last week threatened to raise tariffs back to 25%, underscoring political
and policy uncertainty. The threat appeared to be more about pressuring Seoul to
accelerate legislative approval of the 2025 trade agreement than signalling a breakdown in
bilateral ties.
Exports to China and the U.S. declined in 2025. Favourable base effects flattered gains in
other major markets. Although the trade surplus highlights South Korea’s external
resilience, rising protectionism and slowing global demand warrant caution.
Semiconductor demand, the durability of trade agreements, and volatile global conditions
will determine South Korea's trade trajectory in 2026.
